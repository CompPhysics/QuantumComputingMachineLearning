===== Brief overview of Shor's algorithm =====

o Shor's algorithm is a quantum algorithm for integer factorization.
o It efficiently factors large integers by leveraging quantum period finding.
o Exponential speedup over the best-known classical algorithms.
o Breaks RSA encryption, which relies on the difficulty of factoring.

!split
===== Some history =====

Shor's algorithm, introduced by Peter Shor in 1994, revolutionized the
field of cryptography. It demonstrated that a quantum computer could
solve integer factorization and the discrete logarithm problem
efficiently, posing a significant threat to classical cryptosystems
such as RSA.

!split
===== Problem Statement =====

The objective of Shor's algorithm is to factorize a large composite
integer \( N \) into its prime factors. Given \( N = p \times q \),
where \( p \) and \( q \) are unknown distinct primes, the problem of
factorization can be reduced to finding an integer \( a \) such that
\( 1 < a < N \) and \( \gcd(a, N) = 1 \).

!split
===== Reduction to Order-Finding =====

The key reduction in Shor's algorithm lies in transforming
factorization into an order-finding problem:

o Choose a random integer \( a \) such that \( 1 < a < N \) and \( \gcd(a, N) = 1 \).
o Determine the smallest positive integer \( r \) such that \( a^r \equiv 1 \pmod{N} \).

The integer \( r \) is known as the \textit{order} of \( a \) modulo
\( N \), and it helps in discovering the factors of \( N \).

!split
===== Continued Fraction Expansion =====

After obtaining the rational approximation from the quantum algorithm,
classical computation using continued fraction expansion aids in
deducing the correct order \( r \).



!split
===== Basics of number theory =====

o The problem: Factor an integer $N$ into its prime factors.
o Reduce factoring to period finding $f(x) = a^x \mod N$, where $a$ is randomly chosen such that $\gcd(a, N) = 1$.
o The period $r$ satisfies $a^r \equiv 1 \mod N$.
o Once $r$ is known, factors are given by $\gcd(a^{r/2} \pm 1, N)$.

!split
===== QFTs again =====

The Quantum Fourier Transform is crucial to Shor's algorithm. It
leverages quantum parallelism and interference to efficiently estimate
the periodicity of a function.

===== Quantum Period Finding =====
Quantum Period Finding: Key to Shor's Algorithm

o Quantum subroutine for finding the period $r$ of $f(x)$.
o Similar to Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE).
o Uses two quantum registers:
  * First register: Superposition of all possible inputs $x$.
  * Second register: Computes $f(x) = a^x \mod N$.

!split
===== Applying Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) =====

o The QFT is applied to the first register after the controlled unitary operations.
o Peaks in the measurement correspond to multiples of $1/r$.
o Post-processing classically determines $r$ by continued fractions.



!split
===== Classical Post-Processing =====

o Once $r$ is found, factors of $N$ are computed by $\gcd(a^{r/2} \pm 1, N)$.
o If $r$ is odd or $a^{r/2} \equiv -1 \mod N$, try a different $a$.
o With high probability, this yields a non-trivial factor of $N$.

!split
=====  Complexity and Practical Considerations =====

o Quantum Complexity: Polynomial in $\log N$.
o Classical Best Known: Sub-exponential (Number Field Sieve).
o Quantum Advantage: Exponential speedup over classical algorithms.
o Practical Challenges: Quantum error correction, large qubit counts.


!split
===== Error Analysis and Success Probability =====

The probability that a randomly chosen \( a \) leads to successful
factoring is generally greater than 50\%. The quantum component
requires carefully managed resources for high precision.


Error correction is crucial, as any errors in operations or QFT can
significantly impact the accuracy of period estimation and the
eventual factorization.


!split
===== Summarizing Shor's algorithm =====

Shor's algorithm is a central  quantum algorithm demonstrating
exponential speedup over classical counterparts. It brought both
excitement for potential quantum advancements and a reconsideration of
current cryptographic standards.

o Shor's algorithm is a groundbreaking quantum algorithm for factoring.
o Combines quantum period finding with classical number theory.
o Highlights the potential of quantum computing to break RSA.
